PeptArm

A versatile platform for biomolecular condensate engineering

Amir M. CHERAGHALI, Amir PANDI

INSERM U1338 (ERL), Department of Computational, Quantitative and Synthetic Biology (CQSB), UMR 7238, CNRS-Sorbonne Université, France

Phaseek preprint → All references Download poster (PDF)

Biomolecular condensates are membrane-less compartments formed by liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) that organize proteins.

Key message: PeptArm recruits untagged proteins into synthetic condensates in bacteria and yeast, with no genome editing and no tags.

Overview

Bottleneck: Protein capture by genome editing

Traditional condensate engineering requires fusing condensate-forming tags to proteins, which can disrupt their structure and function.

For endogenous proteins it is even more challenging, since it requires genome editing to insert tags1: a labor- and time-intensive approach that can also interfere with gene expression.

Solution: Binding coupled to phase separation

PeptArm is a modular, bifunctional design that bypasses genome editing.

A specific Peptide Binder (the hook) is fused to a Phase-Separating (PS) Arm (the anchor), designed by our ML model Phaseek2. PeptArms recruit target proteins into membrane-less condensates for non-covalent, tag-free spatial control via LLPS.

Binders tested: NbALFA6, SYNZIP7 and de novo RFdiffusion binders3 (the latter shown on the poster).

How PeptArm works

1

Phase separation
3

Results

Prokaryote, Gram-negative bacterium: Escherichia coli

BL21*T7p · IPTG2 plasmids

Three PS peptide arms (Phaseek), each fused to the RFdiffusion binder, recruit untagged sfGFP into polar condensates, while the control without a PS arm stays diffuse, confirming tag-free capture in a prokaryote.

Eukaryote, budding yeast: Saccharomyces cerevisiae

BY4700 / BY4709GAL1p · galactoseGenome-integrated

In yeast, the same AI-designed binder strategy recruits untagged sfGFP into discrete condensates (arrowheads), while the control stays diffuse, showing the design carries over to a eukaryote.

Microscopy and structure. The phase-contrast, GFP and merged panels (2 µm scale bars) and the AlphaFold prediction of the sfGFP–binder–Phaseek complex are shown on the poster.
